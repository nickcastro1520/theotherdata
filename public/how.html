How it works

"Alternative data" is any information about a company that doesn't come from the company's own financial reports. Hedge funds spend heavily on it: credit-card panels, satellite photos, app downloads. The Other Data uses only free, public, legitimately accessible sources and explains each one like you'd explain it to a friend.

1. Collect

A scheduled GitHub Actions job runs every 4 hours. It calls each source's official API or public download (with a descriptive User-Agent, polite request spacing, and respect for robots.txt), then writes the results as static files that this site reads. Nothing requires a login and nothing is scraped from behind a paywall. Sources that update monthly or quarterly (like Census retail sales or SEC filings) simply show the same numbers until the agency publishes new ones.

2. Compare

  • Fast data (daily pageviews, downloads, prices): the last 28 days' average vs the 28 days before.
  • Weekly data (gas prices, jobless claims, developer chatter): the last 4 weeks vs the 4 weeks before.
  • Seasonal data (retail sales, carloads, FDA reports, TSA lines): this period vs the same period last year, so holidays don't fool us.
  • Snapshot data (job-board counts): we save a snapshot every refresh and build the history ourselves. Until there's enough history, the card says "Tracking".

Each signal has a noise band (for example ±8% for Wikipedia views, ±2% for TSA traffic). Moves inside it are called neutral. Outside it, the move is a tailwind or headwind depending on how that data has typically related to the company (rising jet fuel is bad for an airline, rising oil is good for an oil producer). Some signals cut both ways, so we label them context.

Color tip: green numbers and charts mean the underlying figure is up vs its recent baseline; red means down. Tailwind / Headwind badges are a separate layer that interpret whether that move looks favorable or unfavorable for the company.

Seventeen curated stocks get deep, company-specific signals. Any other US ticker can be looked up for a light pack: live quote and chart, company news, Wikipedia curiosity, Hacker News chatter, and SEC inventory when available — not the same rich custom set as the curated names.

3. Explain

Every signal card answers three questions: what the data is, why it might matter for this company, and what it's showing now. The "showing now" text is generated from the actual numbers by a template. Each stock page also shows a plain-English impact call — what the combined signals lean toward right now (tailwind, headwind, or mixed) — kept educational, never a buy/sell tip. An optional AI summary (Gemini) can rewrite that call from the computed facts and is rejected if it sounds like investment advice.

Ideas vs live proxies

Some classic alt-data stories (parking-lot cars from space, warehouse moths, municipal e-waste tonnage) do not have a free, frequent public feed. Where a legal free stand-in exists — for example weekly EIA crude stocks instead of paid satellite tank estimates, or Federal Reserve paperboard production instead of city recycling tonnage — we wire it into a curated stock and label the idea as live or partial on the home page. Everything else stays an idea.

Correlation scout

A script (scripts/correlation-scout.mjs) periodically scans free public series (FRED, EIA, Wikipedia, PyPI, …) against curated stock prices from Tiingo. It scores Spearman correlations on weekly or monthly returns, tests a short lead window, and writes public/data/scout.json plus a markdown report. We only promote pairings that are wireable for free and have a plain-English economic story — and we still label correlation ≠ causation. Hits that look like pure data dredging stay in the report with a spurious-risk flag.

Honesty rules

  • No made-up numbers. If a source fails, the card says "Data unavailable", or shows the last good data with a clear "stale" warning and the time it was fetched.
  • Ideas without a live data source (like moth counts or recycling volumes) are labeled "Idea · not live".
  • Stock prices only appear when a licensed price feed (Finnhub for quotes, Tiingo for end-of-day history) is connected. We don't scrape prices from sites that disallow it. Otherwise each stock page links out to a price chart.
  • Correlation isn't causation. Public data is often already priced in by the time it's published.
