Filing watch · updated daily

Recent late filers

Education only. Not advice. Being on this list is not a prediction for any one company, and nothing here says what to buy or sell.

When a US public company can't file its annual report (10-K) or quarterly report (10-Q) on time, it has to tell the SEC with a short notice called Form 12b-25, listed on EDGAR as NT 10-K or NT 10-Q. Common reasons: the auditors need more time, the books are being restated, or the company is short on cash or staff.

What we found when we tested it

We took every late-filing notice from 2012 to 2026 that maps to a stock still listed today and checked how the stock did against the S&P 500 afterward, starting the trading day after the notice was filed. To pass, late filers had to trail the S&P 500 more often than a baseline: the better of always guessing "beats the S&P 500" or always guessing "trails it" for the same stocks in the same years. The low end of the 95% range also had to clear that bar.

Read this before reading the list

  • This is a known effect. Finance researchers have documented weak returns after late-filing notices for years. It is not a new discovery, and well-known patterns tend to fade.
  • Survivorship bias. Only companies still listed today could be matched to a ticker, so many late filers that later delisted, often the worst outcomes, are missing from the test.
  • Mostly small, thinly traded stocks. Many are tiny or trade over the counter, where prices can jump on little volume.
  • Costs and limits. Trading costs, wide spreads and limits on short selling would eat into an average like this. A pattern on paper is not a strategy.
  • One notice is not a verdict. Some companies file late for dull reasons and catch up within days.
