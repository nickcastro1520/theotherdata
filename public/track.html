Every curated stock gets an overall lean from its offbeat signals: tailwind, headwind, mixed, or quiet. We log those leans and check what the stock actually did 2 weeks, 1 month, and 1 quarter later, scored two ways: up/down right (did the stock move the way the lean pointed?) and beat the market (did it do better than the S&P 500 over the same time? This is the tougher, fairer test), plus beat its sector (the same test against the company's sector fund, like XLK for tech). Every single signal is scored on its own on the signal scoreboard.

Worth watching, not proven Not signals

These are not signals and not advice. Both failed our admission test (they had to lift the out-of-sample beat-the-market rate by at least 1 point), so they don't affect any lean on this site. They're here because the story is real and the early numbers are interesting.

Generac and power outages

When more homes lose power, more people think about buying a backup generator, and Generac is the biggest maker. We counted customers hit by major outages in the U.S. Department of Energy's OE-417 reports over the past 90 days and compared that with the same 90 days a year earlier. When outages were far above last year, we called it good for Generac; far below, bad.

Across 29 monthly calls (Jan 2023 to Dec 2025), Generac beat the S&P 500 the next month 69% of the time. The 95% range is 50.8% to 82.7%, which only just clears a coin flip. The big catch: the DOE publishes these yearly files after the year ends, so nobody could have used this data in real time. This shows the link exists, not that you could have traded on it.

Compass Minerals and snowfall

Compass sells road salt to cities and states, so a snowy winter should mean more salt sold. That part is clearly true: snowfall at six big Midwest and Northeast airports (NOAA daily weather data) lines up with the highway deicing tons Compass reports each quarter (correlation 0.79 across 18 winter quarters, 2017 to 2026).

The stock is a different story. Calling Compass from season-to-date snow beat the S&P 500 62.5% of the time, but on only 16 calls, and the range still includes a coin flip. Winter only comes once a year, so this test gets 1 new data point per year. It will take many winters to know.

Can we beat a coin flip? Out-of-sample test

      Read this before trusting any number above

      • Small samples. Per-stock results rest on a few dozen calls at most. A 60% hit rate on 30 calls can easily be luck.
      • Overlapping windows. Checkpoints are monthly, so 1-quarter windows overlap and the real number of independent results is smaller than the count shown.
      • Hindsight in the stock list. These stocks and their signals were chosen in 2026, knowing how the companies turned out. That flatters the "vs S&P" column for every lean, which is why tailwind and headwind leans can both show positive average moves.
      • The backtest is not the live site. It uses only the signals with dated public history (listed above), conservative publication delays for most sources, and, for Federal Reserve (FRED) series, the numbers exactly as published at the time (ALFRED vintages), not today's revised data.
      • Correlation isn't causation. A lean that lined up with a price move doesn't mean the data caused it. Public data is often already priced in by the time it's published.
      • Education only. Not financial advice. Nothing here tells anyone to buy, sell, or hold anything.
